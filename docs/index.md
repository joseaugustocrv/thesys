## The problem Thesys addresses

Software can be produced faster than teams can maintain shared understanding of it. Requirements change, decisions become implicit, modules become interconnected, and implementation can drift from the behavior people originally intended. AI increases both the opportunity and the speed of this process.

Thesys addresses that problem by making engineering intent, authority, relationships, verification and evidence explicit parts of the lifecycle.

## The engineering loop

```text
Intent
  ↓
Requirements
  ↓
Specification
  ↓
Architecture & Design
  ↓
Plan
  ↓
Implementation
  ↓
Verification
  ↓
Convergence
  ↓
Release
  ↓
Operation & Evolution
  ↺
```

The lifecycle is not a rigid waterfall. Engineering work can iterate, branch, recurse into smaller units, or return to an earlier source of intent when new information changes the expected result.

## What Thesys emphasizes

- **Traceability** — significant decisions and behavior remain followable across engineering work.
- **Human authority** — consequential engineering decisions remain attributable to people.
- **Risk-proportional rigor** — engineering depth reflects complexity, impact and risk.
- **Recursive scalability** — the same model can be applied across systems, domains, modules and smaller units.
- **AI as an engineering participant** — AI can analyze and propose, while authoritative decisions remain under the project's governance model.

## A methodology for real systems

Thesys is intended for more than isolated greenfield features. The model can be applied to new systems, existing systems, defects, migrations, architectural initiatives, security work, technical debt and operationally driven changes.

The common foundation remains the same: understand the intended outcome, make relevant decisions explicit, preserve traceability, verify the result and control subsequent change.
